    What each question costs

    The knowledge is paid for once when it's loaded, then read from the cache at a tenth of the price for every question.

    Billed input in tokens, at Claude's usual rates. From the second question on, CAG is cheaper; by the tenth, over 75% cheaper overall. In the CAG paper, answering from a precomputed cache took 2.26 s versus 92 s when an 85,000-token context was read from scratch each time (Llama 3.1 8B, HotPotQA).

    Why teams like CAG

    It is the simplest way to give a model your knowledge, and often good enough.

    No retrieval pipelineNo splitting documents into chunks, no embeddings, no vector database to build and keep in sync.
    Nothing to missThe model sees every document, so there is no search step that can bring back the wrong passage.
    Answers can combine documentsA question that touches the leave policy and the IT handbook works, because both are in the window.
    Fast and cheap once loadedEach question only processes its own tokens. The knowledge comes from the cache.
    In the paper that named CAG (Chan et al., 2024, "Don't Do RAG"), preloading the documents matched or beat retrieval in 5 of 6 test settings on SQuAD and HotPotQA, with 21,000 to 85,000 tokens of knowledge. The authors note it suits knowledge bases that fit comfortably in the window.

    Using CAG well

    Before reaching for retrieval, get the most out of the window you already have.

    1. Knowledge first, frozenPut it right after the system prompt and never change a byte between requests.
    2. Trim before you loadRemove duplicates, boilerplate and outdated sections. Fewer tokens, sharper answers.
    3. Give it structureHeadings and section IDs let the model find things and cite where an answer came from.
    4. Leave roomKeep space for the conversation history and the longest answer you expect.
    5. Keep the cache warmSteady traffic keeps it alive. For quiet periods, use a longer lifetime or a scheduled keep-alive.
    6. Update in batchesEvery change re-writes the cache from that point on, so refresh on a schedule, not per edit.
    7. Check the cache is usedThe API reports cache-read tokens. If they stay at zero, something early in the prompt is changing.
    8. Know when to stopIf the knowledge outgrows the window or changes constantly, it's time for retrieval.
